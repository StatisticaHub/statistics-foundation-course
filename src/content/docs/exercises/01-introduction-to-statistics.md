:::note[Instructions]

These exercises are designed to test conceptual clarity, mathematical notation, and statistical reasoning. Write full explanations, not only final answers.

:::

## Part A: Conceptual precision

### Question 1

Explain why the following statement is incomplete:

> Statistics is the study of averages.

Your answer should explain what is missing from this definition.

### Question 2

For each situation, state whether the main task is **descriptive** or **inferential**.

| Situation | Descriptive or inferential? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A teacher calculates the mean mark of the 35 students in her class. |  |  |
| A polling agency surveys 1,200 voters to estimate national support for a party. |  |  |
| A hospital summarises the waiting times of patients treated last Monday. |  |  |
| A researcher uses 500 sampled adults to estimate the average blood pressure of adults in a city. |  |  |
| A company reports the total sales from all stores last month. |  |  |

### Question 3

Explain the difference between **data**, **information**, and **statistical evidence**.

Give one example where a number becomes meaningful only after context is provided.

## Part B: Mathematical reasoning

### Question 4

Suppose the population values are:

$$
X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_N.
$$

Write the expression for the population mean $\mu$.

Then explain why $\mu$ is usually unknown in real statistical studies.

### Question 5

Suppose a sample contains:

$$
x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n.
$$

Write the expression for the sample mean $\bar{x}$.

Explain why $\bar{x}$ may differ from $\mu$.

### Question 6

A population has values:

$$
4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12.
$$

1. Calculate the population mean $\mu$.
2. Suppose a sample of size 3 is selected: $4,\ 8,\ 12$. Calculate $\bar{x}$.
3. Suppose another sample of size 3 is selected: $4,\ 6,\ 8$. Calculate $\bar{x}$.
4. Explain what this demonstrates about sampling variation.

## Part C: Applied interpretation

### Question 7

A researcher measures the systolic blood pressure of 100 adults in Bristol and obtains:

$$
\bar{x}=128.
$$

Answer the following:

1. What does 128 represent?
2. Is it a parameter or a statistic?
3. What population parameter might it estimate?
4. What additional information would you need before trusting this estimate?

### Question 8

A student says:

> Since the sample mean is 128, the population mean is definitely 128.

Explain why this statement is statistically incorrect.

## Part D: Challenge problems

### Question 9

Construct an example where two different samples from the same population produce different sample means.

Your example must include:

- a population of at least 6 values;
- two different samples of size 3;
- the mean of each sample;
- a short explanation of sampling variation.

### Question 10

A city council wants to estimate the proportion of households that recycle regularly.

They collect data only from households that attend an environmental awareness event.

Explain why this may produce a biased estimate.

Your answer should distinguish between:

- sample size;
- sample representativeness;
- sampling bias.

## Part E: Proof-style reasoning

### Question 11

Explain why probability is needed for inferential statistics but not always needed for purely descriptive statistics.

### Question 12

Complete the following conceptual chain:

$$
\text{Observed data}
\rightarrow
\text{sample statistic}
\rightarrow
\text{population parameter}
\rightarrow
\text{uncertainty statement}.
$$

Explain each arrow in words.

## Answer check

<details>
<summary>Show brief answer guidance</summary>

1. Statistics is not only averages; it includes data collection, variation, uncertainty, inference, and interpretation.  
2. Descriptive tasks summarise observed data; inferential tasks generalise beyond observed data.  
3. A statistic such as $\bar{x}$ estimates a parameter such as $\mu$, but sampling variation means they need not be equal.  
4. Sampling bias occurs when the sample selection process systematically excludes or overrepresents certain parts of the population.  

</details>
