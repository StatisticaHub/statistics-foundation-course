:::note[Instructions]

For each question, define the statistical objects precisely. Avoid vague answers such as “people” or “data” unless you specify exactly which people or which data.

:::

## Part A: Identification exercises

### Question 1

A university wants to estimate the average weekly study time of all first-year undergraduate students. A questionnaire is sent to 500 first-year students, and 210 respond.

Identify:

1. target population;
2. sampling frame;
3. realised sample;
4. observational unit;
5. main variable;
6. possible source of bias.

### Question 2

A hospital records waiting time for every patient visit to its emergency department during one week.

Identify:

1. population, if the hospital wants to describe only that week;
2. population, if the hospital wants to generalise to future weeks;
3. observational unit;
4. variable;
5. whether the data are a census or sample in each interpretation.

### Question 3

A researcher studies 50 schools and records the average exam score in each school.

What is the observational unit?

Explain why the observational unit is not an individual student in this case.

## Part B: Mathematical notation

### Question 4

Let the population be:

$$
\mathcal{P}=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}.
$$

A sample is:

$$
\mathcal{S}=\{i_1,i_2,\ldots,i_n\}.
$$

Explain the meaning of:

$$
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}.
$$

### Question 5

Suppose the variable measured on unit $i$ is $X_i$.

Explain the difference between:

$$
X_i
$$

and

$$
x_i.
$$

### Question 6

A population contains $N=10,000$ adults. A sample contains $n=250$ adults.

Write a short mathematical description of the sample as a subset of the population.

### Question 8

A study asks 1,000 people at a railway station whether they support a new public transport policy.

Discuss whether this sample is likely to represent the whole city population.

Your answer should mention:

- location bias;
- time-of-day effects;
- exclusion of people who do not use trains;
- why a large sample does not automatically solve bias.

## Part D: Challenge problems

### Question 9

Create two different research questions using the same dataset:

Dataset: 5,000 hospital admissions with patient age, diagnosis, length of stay, and discharge status.

For each research question, identify:

- population;
- observational unit;
- outcome or main variable;
- whether the question is descriptive or inferential.

### Question 10

A researcher says:

> My sample contains 10,000 people, so it must be representative.

Write a critical response explaining why this claim is not necessarily true.

## Part E: Short proof-style reasoning

### Question 11

Explain why the definition of the observational unit must come before the definition of the variable.

### Question 12

A dataset contains repeated blood pressure measurements for each patient.

Discuss two possible observational units:

1. patient;
2. measurement occasion.

Explain how the choice changes the interpretation.

## Answer check

<details>
<summary>Show brief answer guidance</summary>

The key is precision. The population is the full target group, the sample is the observed subset, the observational unit is the unit measured, and the variable is the characteristic recorded. Large samples may still be biased if the selection mechanism is not representative.

</details>
