## Part A: Frequency tables

### Question 1

A sample of 80 patients is classified by blood group.

| Blood group | Frequency |
|---|---:|
| A | 22 |
| B | 18 |
| AB | 8 |
| O | 32 |

Calculate:

1. relative frequency for each blood group;
2. percentage for each blood group;
3. modal blood group;
4. whether a bar chart or histogram is appropriate.

### Question 2

A variable has categories $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$ with frequencies:

$$
12,\ 18,\ 25,\ 45.
$$

1. Find the total sample size.
2. Calculate the relative frequency of each category.
3. Calculate the percentage of each category.
4. Check that the relative frequencies sum to 1.

## Part C: Interpretation from grouped data

### Question 6

Exam marks are grouped as follows.

| Mark interval | Frequency |
|---|---:|
| 0–20 | 2 |
| 20–40 | 6 |
| 40–60 | 14 |
| 60–80 | 20 |
| 80–100 | 8 |

Answer:

1. What is the modal interval?
2. Is the distribution concentrated more at low, middle, or high marks?
3. Would a histogram be appropriate?
4. What information is lost when raw data are grouped?
