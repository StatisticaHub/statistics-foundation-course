## Part A: Classification

### Question 1

Classify each question as descriptive or inferential.

| Question | Descriptive or inferential? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| What is the mean age of patients in this dataset? |  |  |
| What is the estimated mean age of all patients attending the clinic? |  |  |
| What percentage of sampled households own a car? |  |  |
| Does the sample provide evidence that more than 60% of households own a car? |  |  |
| What is the standard deviation of the observed exam marks? |  |  |
| Is the population mean exam mark different from 50? |  |  |

### Question 2

Explain how the same sample mean can be used descriptively in one context and inferentially in another.

## Part B: Interpretation

### Question 3

A sample of 60 students has mean mark:

$$
\bar{x}=68.
$$

Write one descriptive interpretation and one inferential interpretation.

### Question 4

A researcher reports:

> In our sample, 42% of participants were physically inactive. Therefore, 42% of adults in the country are physically inactive.

Critique this statement.

## Part C: Probability and uncertainty

### Question 5

Explain why inferential statistics requires probability.

Your answer should mention:

- sampling variation;
- sampling distribution;
- uncertainty;
- population parameters.

### Question 6

Before data are observed, the sample mean is:

$$
\bar{X}.
$$

After data are observed, the sample mean is:

$$
\bar{x}.
$$

Explain why this distinction matters for inference.

## Part D: Challenge problems

### Question 7

Construct a scenario where descriptive statistics are sufficient and inference is unnecessary.

Then construct a scenario where inference is essential.

### Question 8

A researcher studies all patients admitted to one hospital in January.

They calculate the mean length of stay.

Discuss whether this is descriptive or inferential under two different research aims:

1. describing January admissions only;
2. generalising to future admissions.

### Question 9

A sample is biased, but the descriptive statistics are calculated correctly.

Explain how both statements can be true.

## Part E: Critical reasoning

### Question 10

Explain why the phrase “the data prove” is often too strong in inferential statistics.

### Question 11

A politician quotes a survey result but does not explain how the sample was selected.

What questions should a statistician ask before trusting the conclusion?

### Question 12

Complete this statement:

> Descriptive statistics summarise ________, while inferential statistics use ________ to learn about ________.

Then explain each blank.

## Answer check

<details>
<summary>Show brief answer guidance</summary>

Descriptive statistics summarise observed data. Inferential statistics generalise from sample to population. Inference requires probability because sample statistics vary from sample to sample.

</details>
