## Part A: Basic spread calculations

### Question 1

For the dataset:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10
$$

calculate:

1. mean;
2. range;
3. deviations from the mean;
4. squared deviations;
5. sample variance;
6. sample standard deviation.

### Question 2

For the dataset:

$$
5,\ 5,\ 5,\ 5,\ 5
$$

calculate the range, sample variance, and sample standard deviation.

Explain why all measures of spread are zero.

### Question 3

For the dataset:

$$
1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 100
$$

calculate the range.

Explain why the range may be misleading.

## Part B: Variance and standard deviation

### Question 4

Explain why the sample variance uses squared deviations instead of raw deviations.

### Question 5

Explain why standard deviation is usually easier to interpret than variance.

### Question 6

For a dataset measured in kilograms, what are the units of:

1. variance;
2. standard deviation?

## Part C: Interquartile range

### Question 7

For the ordered dataset:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 5,\ 7,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12,\ 15,\ 18
$$

find:

1. median;
2. $Q_1$;
3. $Q_3$;
4. IQR.

### Question 8

Explain why the IQR is useful for skewed data.

### Question 9

Compare standard deviation and IQR.

Your answer should mention:

- use of all values;
- sensitivity to outliers;
- suitable contexts.

## Part D: Challenge problems

### Question 10

Create two datasets with the same mean but different standard deviations.

Explain which dataset is more variable.

### Question 11

Create a dataset where the range is large but the IQR is small.

Explain what this suggests about the data.

### Question 12

A class has mean mark 70 and standard deviation 2.

Another class has mean mark 70 and standard deviation 18.

Interpret the difference.

## Part E: Proof-style reasoning

### Question 13

Explain why:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\bar{x})=0
$$

makes raw deviations unsuitable as a direct measure of spread.

### Question 14

Explain the idea of degrees of freedom in the sample variance formula.

## Answer check

<details>
<summary>Show brief answer guidance</summary>

Range uses only extremes. Variance averages squared deviations. Standard deviation is the square root of variance and returns to original units. IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% and is less sensitive to outliers.

</details>
