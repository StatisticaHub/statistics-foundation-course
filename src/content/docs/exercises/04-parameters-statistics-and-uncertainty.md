## Part B: Calculation and interpretation

### Question 4

A population consists of:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10.
$$

Calculate:

1. population mean $\mu$;
2. population variance $\sigma^2$;
3. sample mean for sample $2,\ 6,\ 10$;
4. sample variance for sample $2,\ 6,\ 10$.

### Question 5

A sample of 100 adults gives:

$$
\bar{x}=72,\quad s=8.
$$

Interpret both values.

What population parameters might they estimate?

### Question 6

A sample of 300 voters gives:

$$
\hat{p}=0.58.
$$

1. What does $\hat{p}$ represent?
2. What parameter might it estimate?
3. Why is $\hat{p}$ uncertain?

## Part C: Sampling variation

### Question 7

Create a population of 6 values.

Then choose three different samples of size 3 and calculate their sample means.

Use your example to explain sampling variation.

### Question 8

A researcher says:

> The sample mean is random before sampling but fixed after sampling.

Explain this statement using $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{x}$.

## Part D: Challenge problems

### Question 9

Suppose $\bar{X}$ is used to estimate $\mu$.

Explain why the following statement is incomplete:

> $\bar{X}$ is a good estimator of $\mu$.

What additional properties might we want to investigate?

### Question 10

Consider two sampling methods:

1. simple random sampling;
2. volunteer sampling.

Explain how both can produce a sample mean, but why the uncertainty and bias properties may differ.

## Part E: Proof-style reasoning

### Question 11

Show that if:

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i,
$$

then:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i=n\bar{x}.
$$

### Question 12

Explain why sample statistics are central to inference but parameters are central to scientific questions.

## Answer check

<details>
<summary>Show brief answer guidance</summary>

A parameter describes a population and is usually fixed but unknown. A statistic is calculated from sample data. Before sampling, $\bar{X}$ is random; after sampling, $\bar{x}$ is observed. Sampling variation means different samples can give different statistics.

</details>
