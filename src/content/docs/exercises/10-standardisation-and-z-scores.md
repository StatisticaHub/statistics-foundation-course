## Part A: Basic z-score calculation

### Question 1

A student scores 78 on an exam where:

$$
\bar{x}=70,\quad s=4.
$$

Calculate the z-score and interpret it.

### Question 2

A patient has systolic blood pressure 150 mmHg. In the reference group:

$$
\bar{x}=130,\quad s=10.
$$

Calculate the z-score and interpret it clinically.

### Question 3

A value has z-score:

$$
z=-1.5.
$$

Explain what this means.

## Part B: Comparing values

### Question 4

Student A scores 75 in Exam A, where:

$$
\bar{x}_A=65,\quad s_A=10.
$$

Student B scores 70 in Exam B, where:

$$
\bar{x}_B=55,\quad s_B=5.
$$

1. Calculate each z-score.
2. Which student performed better relative to their class?
3. Explain why raw scores alone are insufficient.

### Question 5

A baby weighs 3.2 kg. The mean birth weight is 3.4 kg with standard deviation 0.4 kg.

Calculate the z-score and interpret it.

## Part C: Theory

### Question 6

Explain why subtracting the mean recentres the data.

### Question 7

Explain why dividing by the standard deviation rescales the data.

### Question 8

If:

$$
Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma},
$$

show that:

$$
E(Z)=0.
$$

### Question 9

If:

$$
Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma},
$$

show that:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(Z)=1.
$$

## Part D: Challenge problems

### Question 10

Create two different raw values from two different distributions that have the same z-score.

Explain what this means.

### Question 11

A value has a very high positive z-score.

Give one context where this is desirable and one context where this is concerning.

### Question 12

Suppose the standard deviation of a dataset is zero.

Explain why z-scores cannot be calculated.

## Part E: Critical reasoning

### Question 13

A student says:

> A z-score removes all interpretation problems because it standardises everything.

Critique this statement.

### Question 14

Explain why standardisation does not automatically make a skewed distribution normal.

### Question 15

A patient’s biomarker value has $z=2.5$ compared with a healthy reference group.

What additional information is needed before deciding whether this is clinically abnormal?

## Answer check

<details>
<summary>Show brief answer guidance</summary>

A z-score is $z=(x-\bar{x})/s$ for sample data. It measures distance from the mean in standard deviation units. Standardisation gives mean 0 and variance 1, but it does not change the fundamental shape of the distribution.

</details>
