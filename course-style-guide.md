# Course Style Guide

This page defines the writing style for the Statistics Foundation course.

---

## Core teaching style

This course is theoretical, descriptive, and concept-focused.

The course avoids programming, R, Python, software tutorials, or coding workflows.

The emphasis is on:

- statistical reasoning;
- mathematical definitions;
- probability foundations;
- derivations;
- interpretation;
- conceptual clarity;
- exam-style thinking.

---

## Recurring characters

The lectures use five recurring characters throughout the course.

| Character | Role |
|---|---|
| Mr. R | Teacher and mentor |
| Oliver | Curious beginner who asks simple but important questions |
| Emma | Careful student who asks for definitions and clarity |
| James | Practical student who asks why concepts matter |
| Sophia | Mathematically strong student who asks about equations and derivations |

---

## Lecture style

Lectures are written as classroom-style conversations.

The purpose of a lecture is to introduce ideas gently before the formal notes.

Each lecture should include:

1. A short scene.
2. A natural conversation between Mr. R and the students.
3. Simple examples.
4. Misconceptions and clarifications.
5. A gentle mathematical bridge.
6. A final summary.

The lecture should feel friendly, human, and easy to follow.

---

## Notes style

Notes are formal, descriptive, and theoretical.

Each note should include:

1. Learning objectives.
2. Formal definitions.
3. Mathematical notation.
4. Important equations.
5. Derivations where relevant.
6. Interpretation.
7. Common mistakes.
8. Summary.

The notes should be deeper and more rigorous than the lectures.

---

## Exercises style

Exercises should test statistical understanding, not coding ability.

Exercises may include:

- definitions;
- conceptual questions;
- mathematical derivations;
- interpretation questions;
- short proof-style questions;
- applied but non-computational examples.

---

## Examples style

Examples should be real-world but non-coding.

Examples should focus on:

- identifying populations and samples;
- interpreting statistical quantities;
- understanding uncertainty;
- connecting formulas to real situations;
- explaining statistical ideas in words.
